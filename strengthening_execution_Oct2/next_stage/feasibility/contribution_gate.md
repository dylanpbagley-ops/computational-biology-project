# Contribution gate — final decision

**Decision: no demonstrated strong extension.** The completed GSE305971 run provides a useful, measured within-study assay-policy comparison, but its differences are largely expected from the policy constraints, are uneven by marker, and do not establish clinical utility, new antigen selectivity, or independent transportability. Keep it as supporting computational evidence; do not present it as a new biological discovery.

1. **Exact new claim.** On a locked first-biopsy, bone-marrow MARS subset, fitting a worst-code healthy-reference RNA budget can reduce held-out healthy-cell positivity and malignant-cell transcript detection relative to pooled/equal-code rules for TNFRSF17 and SLAMF7. For GPRC5D, all three policies select the same cutoff and yield the same measurements. The run does not establish that any policy is clinically better.
2. **Closest work.** The 2026 GSE305971 study already performs malignant-versus-`Normal_PC` target discovery; the existing source review records a per-patient z-score ranking. The Nature Genetics article and its GSE305971 Data Availability record identify the study and target-discovery scope. Direct access to the full Methods/Figure route and runnable author scripts was unavailable in this pass, so the exact z-score formula and reference pooling were not replicated. Prior-art overlap is therefore treated conservatively, and the transparent paired mean contrast is not called a head-to-head baseline.
3. **Opened usable data.** Yes. We retrieved the public annotation workbook and all 456 selected processed count tables (306,858,728 compressed bytes). After strict integer-count checks and keyed `(cell ID, Amp.Batch.ID)` joins, 62,811 of 65,508 selected annotations were usable. This is a completed same-study code-held-out analysis, not a second independent cohort.
4. **Independent units and confounders.** `Hospital.Code` is a putative-person grouping key, not a verified global person ID. The 8 training Healthy codes and the 9 new Healthy/90 new MM evaluation codes are disjoint by that key; GSE193531 patient non-overlap is unknown. The workbook provides `Amp.Batch.ID` and `Seq.Batch.ID`, and the exact join uses `Amp.Batch.ID`; these identify assay/processing partitions but do not separate batch from biology. The count join is partial (2,697 unmatched selected IDs, concentrated in MM codes); age, sex, preparation, immunoglobulin composition and matched cell-composition covariates are incomplete or unavailable. A source-wide 668 Healthy/Malignant-label cells remain biologically ambiguous; 659 joined selected cells were kept as a separate uncertain-label sensitivity, never called normal or malignant.
5. **Disconfirmation and counterevidence.** GPRC5D has no policy difference. TNFRSF17's envelope reduces Healthy positives from 102/2,304 (4.43%) to 14/2,304 (0.61%) and MM positives from 12,127/44,916 (27.00%) to 5,992/44,916 (13.34%); the separation survives worst-case MM unmatched-cell bounds, but the policy ordering follows from stricter constraints. SLAMF7's envelope gives 44/2,304 Healthy and 2,681/44,916 MM positives versus 153/2,304 and 5,336/44,916 under pooled fitting, while missingness bounds nearly touch. Healthy code violations include small denominators and coarse one-cell steps; several one-cell violations are not population-level reliability evidence. These results weaken a strong-extension claim.
6. **Fair baseline and evaluation.** All policies used the same 8 pilot-Healthy training codes, the same reference candidates and budgets, and the untouched 9 Healthy plus 90 MM putative codes for primary expansion; all 16 pilot MM codes were excluded. Paired within-code malignant-minus-`Normal_PC` means were descriptive only. The paper's published z-score method was not reconstructed, no patient-level uncertainty was inferred from cells, and no AUC/significance/safety endpoint was calculated. Parent independently recomputed all four budgets across 7,632 primary and uncertain-label policy rows, including thresholds and positive counts/denominators, and all 3,816 missingness bounds.
7. **January deliverable.** A carefully scoped computational report with its protocol, source records, evidence-linked figures and explanation checks is feasible by the school fair deadline. This can document completed research, but this result does not itself meet the requested bar of a demonstrated substantial biological/medical extension or support claims about treatment safety. Student-authored explanation and school/adult review remain necessary; no clinical reviewer is currently identified.
8. **Retained assets and new work.** The original 63 protected inputs, prior analysis outputs, and frozen protocols are preserved. New assets include verified GSE305971 retrieval and join audit, a locked exposure-aware analysis, per-code thresholds/coverage/missingness tables, a limited clinical evidence normalization, and supporting figures. No original labels, predictions, clinical classifications, or submitted prose were changed.

**Gate outcome:** B fails as the primary substantive extension; F1 and F2 also fail as primary branches in the bounded screens. Select exactly one path: **no demonstrated strong extension**, while retaining the B policy audit and F1/F2 evidence as supporting work. Remaining dependencies are source-code/method access for an exact prior-art implementation, verified cross-study patient identity, resolution of unmatched cell IDs and source label ambiguity, and independent clinical endpoint adjudication. None is inferred solved here.
