# Research contribution assessment

**A strong new biological or medical contribution was not demonstrated.** The completed work supplies a reproducible audit of how three healthy-reference RNA threshold policies behave in the public GSE305971 MARS assay. Under a 10% training reference budget, the donor-envelope rule trades lower held-out Healthy positivity for lower MM transcript detection on TNFRSF17 and SLAMF7. GPRC5D is unchanged because every policy selects the same threshold. The TNFRSF17 difference is robust to worst-case treatment of unmatched annotations; SLAMF7 is less secure at its missingness and code-resolution limits.

The strongest counterargument is that the donor-envelope ordering is partly a mathematical consequence of stricter constraints, while the paper already ranks targets using patient-specific malignant-versus-`Normal_PC` analyses. Small healthy code sizes make one-cell changes large, and the source labels, putative participant identity, unmatched IDs, and assay-only measurement constrain interpretation. The result does not establish that the donor-envelope policy is superior, clinically useful, or safer, and it adds no antigen-discovery claim.

The most defensible use is supporting evidence that assay-level donor-budget choices can change observed transcript-detection tradeoffs in this selected source dataset. The local GSE193531 depth pilot adds a separate same-cell measurement-sensitivity check. The clinical table now preserves source event counts and grades as descriptive facts but leaves endpoint observation states indeterminate pending independent clinical review. No labels, predictions, or clinical AUC were changed.

For a January fair, a student may present a methods-focused report of this completed test, including its negative novelty decision and limits, if they can explain the analysis and the advisor accepts the scope. It does not meet the project's requested threshold for a demonstrated substantial extension. This assessment is not a submission-ready paper, abstract, poster, or research plan; those remain student-authored.
