# Decision: do not select a functional extension from these two candidates

Neither screened question currently supports a defensible, substantial extension. Reject candidate A because its chosen pre-exposure growth-history to later ampicillin-survival contrast is already directly tested in the closest paper. Reject candidate B for this stage because the only remaining possible increment is a narrow retrospective panel-resampling analysis whose novelty and independent measured-outcome evaluation have not been established. This does not show that no future extension exists; it means neither candidate has earned a pilot under the agreed gates.

For candidate A, Umetani et al. (2025) Fig. 7B-C directly compare the fraction of cells that neither grow nor divide before exposure with subsequent ampicillin survivor frequency. Equation 1 uses `f = s P_n`; the paper estimates `s` from the late-stationary condition and tests the relation across the reported conditions. This is the exact predictor/outcome contrast selected for the gate. Calling the proposed work a comparison against a mean-only baseline would not add a distinct biological question. The novelty gate therefore fails; further antibiotic data access and a pilot were stopped.

For candidate B, a precisely bounded proposal would select one antibody pair from a fixed candidate set using baseline single-bnAb susceptibility in a finite pseudovirus panel, then evaluate empirical combo IC80 coverage on viruses excluded from selection. Wagh et al. (2016) is the key measured-data source: it reports ten experimental combinations on twenty viruses, but those viruses were deliberately selected for sensitivity to almost all tested bnAbs and to span a maximized IC80 range (Methods/Results; Fig. 2). The paper's 1,622 combination scores across the wider 200-virus panel are Bliss-Hill predictions, not measured combination outcomes. Its measured subset is too selected to stand in for rare-resistance-tail or general clinical coverage without a separate, defensible evaluation design and isolate/donor grouping.

Williamson et al. (2024) does not close that empirical gap: individual bnAb susceptibility values are observed, while Fig. 4's combination IC50/IC80 values are calculated from the singles under additive or Bliss-Hill models. Its held-out cross-validation and sample-size simulations are useful prior art for prediction/sampling methodology, but do not independently validate measured combination neutralization or finite-panel treatment-choice stability. LaMont et al. (2022) further shows that genetic susceptibility has already been used to model clinical rebound and rank cocktails. The local HIVTreatmentOptimization HDF5 preserves seven patient genetic records and seven matching viraemic trace records (10–19 paired values per patient), alongside fitted x/k and growth/decay values. Repository code derives rebound times as -log(x)/growth and simulates/ranks therapies from genetic inputs. Those trace inputs are empirical clinical data; derived rebound values and rankings are model-based. This file is not a matched combination-neutralization cohort or an independent holdout for the assay-level question selected here.

The public data distinction matters. Measured combination outcomes exist in Wagh's selected twenty-virus subset, and the paper says its data are in the article/supporting files. The local supplement extraction produced one 21-row by 7-column summary table of coverage percentages, not linked isolate-level single-plus-combination rows. The author repository identifies public CATNAP individual-antibody data, but Williamson's combination outcomes are calculated rather than measured. The exact matched-row schema, censoring, donor/lineage groups and a representative independent holdout were not reconstructed. The separate HDF5 has clinical trace inputs, but it does not link measured combo IC50/IC80 outcomes to the finite-panel question. Therefore B is not rejected because no public measurements exist; its exact novelty/comparator and evaluation basis remain insufficient for selection.

No pilot ran because neither candidate passed both gates. No new dataset, model result, or independent validation is claimed. The appropriate next decision is a broader pivot decision in a separately scoped stage: either complete the current project as its existing completed work or define a materially different functional question with a measured endpoint, a genuinely unresolved decision increment, and independent outcome groups before implementation. This stage does not nominate a third nearby candidate. Keep the previously verified myeloma result and all old labels/predictions/files unchanged.

Student explanation points:

- The antibiotic paper already measures the proposed pre-exposure fraction and later survival relationship.
- Some HIV combination measurements are real, but the measured Wagh panel has only twenty deliberately selected viruses; the broader combination rankings are predictions.
- Another HIV paper measures individual antibodies but calculates combination responses from those values; predictions or rebound simulations are not measured combo outcomes.
- The current project remains completed prior work. This bounded search found no extension that is ready to claim or pilot.
