# Cycle 2 decision

## Decision

Neither C02 nor C05 yields a new pilot candidate from this bounded feasibility review. This stops the current candidate questions; it does not imply either biological field is exhausted. C05 has real data that link measured tumor-MS peptides to measured T-cell responses, but the studied contrasts are selected and already published; they do not establish natural tumor-cell recognition by a patient's own T cells. No distinct data-backed revision clears novelty and evaluation.

## Branch A: acute drug response and durable regrowth

The broad acute-response-to-regrowth question has strong primary-study precedent. PROSPERO (2023, PMID 37171617) links acute single-cell molecular measurements to later cytotoxicity. Stationary-to-proliferating lineage tracking (Mizrahi 2016, PMID 27801609) measures post-treatment divisions after washout. The most decisive comparator is the 2025 patient-derived breast CTC study (PMID 40623195): a 10 nM docetaxel 16-hour pulse is followed by drug-free monitoring to 11 weeks. Eighteen cultures were classified as 12 perpro and 6 perdef; BRx-168 was intrinsically resistant and excluded from later analyses, leaving 17. The paper reports about 7 weeks median regrowth time and 4.4% median residual viable fraction, plus cell-cycle/ploidy and CDKN1B/p27 findings.

Parent source review confirms that this paper already compares baseline CDKN1A/B between perpro and perdef; its supplementary analysis links low baseline CDKN1B in a perpro subset with >=8N ploidy and 1-Emax; and post-pulse AKT inhibition reduces persister fraction and delays regrowth. Those contrasts are closed. The GEO count matrices are expression files, not a verified table pairing early cell-death measurements with long-term fate. A bounded public counts-CSV request returned HTTP 403; no rows were retained. GR metrics (Hafner 2016) address division-rate confounding, and fractional-killing assays (Inde 2020) measure death kinetics during treatment, but neither substitutes for reproductive fate after washout. Engineered caspase imaging (Nano 2023) follows reporter-positive cells for about 20.5 hours, not long-term tumor regrowth; a competing apoptosis paper was available only at abstract level.

A specific early-MOMP/caspase-commitment-versus-long-term-regrowth question remains biologically interesting, but it is only one possible narrowing of C02. No matched accessible data or distinct increment for it was verified. The broader C02 question also lacks a new mechanism beyond the published CTC and lineage work. No pilot or new revision is recommended.

## Branch B: natural tumor presentation and T-cell recognition

The generic presentation-versus-recognition distinction is established. TESLA (Wells 2020; PMID 33038342) tested patient-matched loaded-pMHC T-cell recognition, but its “presented” class is a filter over measured affinity and tumor expression plus predicted binding stability, not direct MS detection of natural tumor ligands. BigMHC (Albert 2023; PMID 37829001) separates MS-ligand presentation training from immunogenicity assay sources; that work does not establish same-patient paired tumor-MS and T-cell labels.

A direct measured ex-vivo link does exist. Ely et al. (Science 2025; PMID 40339010) report LC-MS/MS-observed peptides from PDAC tumor organoids and test selected engineered TCRs for endogenous PDO recognition and killing, including P0071. Low- versus high-avidity recognition is already examined in that paper. The use of engineered T cells is a limitation for patient-autologous claims, but does not disqualify the narrower biological finding that a naturally observed PDO ligand can be recognized endogenously by a selected TCR. The official supplement was inaccessible (HTTP 403), so its row keys were not inspected.

The JCI breast work provides an additional actual peptide-level join. Its publisher-hosted Supporting Data Values workbook (25,450,629 bytes) and Supplemental Table 5 workbook (15,298 bytes) were downloaded and checked. Table 5 contains 74 antigen records and 12 populated fields, including peptide sequence, tumor-sample field, HLA allele, predicted Repitope score, mTEC transcript mean and HLA Ligand Atlas flag. Eleven peptide sequences overlap exactly with the Figure 6 functional T-cell sheets. Figure 6 reports peptide-pulsed CD8 clonotype frequencies and fold changes; the article used PBMCs from two healthy donors. Figure 6 has no donor ID or tumor-recognition field, and clonotypes/rows are not independent donors. The Table 5 tumor-sample field is inconsistent: some multiple sample IDs are comma-separated, while some appear as numeric decimals (for example EADPTGHSY 1029.1478 and DEFNGVPSY 825.21789999999999). Those values were not reinterpreted as patient lists. The selected 11-of-74 assays support a peptide-level measured pairing but not an unambiguous per-patient natural-presentation-to-recognition join. Peptide pulsing bypasses natural processing and does not demonstrate recognition of an unmanipulated tumor. The paper states that TMT improved peptide identification and was not used for quantification; no peptide abundance claim is drawn from it.

Kina et al. also test CAF-derived antigens and normal-tissue expression, and Table 5 includes mTEC expression and HLA Ligand Atlas flags. The article already reports that none of its aeTSAs appear in the HLA Ligand Atlas and compares normal-tissue expression; the later Science PDO study also filters candidate peptides against healthy tissue. A generic external-atlas or normal-tissue safety filter is therefore not a new increment. epiVIP (2026 preprint) links MS/transcriptome training data to functional cohorts using imputed abundance and predictions; those outputs are not direct paired measurements for every clinical peptide. MS non-detection is not a negative natural-presentation label.

The initial matched-stage question has relevant measured precedents, including the Science endogenous-PDO assay and the JCI peptide-level join. It stops because the strongest direct contrast is already published and no separate data-backed increment passed review. No narrower candidate is recommended from this selected dataset.

## Access, reading, and limits

Eight PubMed-connector full-text articles had relevant methods/results read; two additional primary sources (stationary-to-proliferating transition and SCLC relapse) received targeted methods/results inspection. The JCI supplementary workbooks were inspected at row/schema level: all 74 Table 5 records; all 63 Figure 6A rows; the 564 Figure 6B rows were parsed and summarized by peptide-condition; and 20 Figure 6C plus 188 Figure 6D clonotype rows. The remaining 20 DataValues sheets had dimensions and first-row samples inspected. Total focused processed rows parsed in the five detailed tables: 909, with clonotypes nested and not independent biological replicates. Branch A processed row inspections: 0. Two files were retained locally (25,465,927 bytes total). CTC GEO and the Science supplement returned HTTP 403; TESLA spreadsheet access stopped at a signed-download redirect. These outcomes do not prove uninspected files lack fields.

No pilot is specified. The JCI peptide-level join is the most actionable resource inspected, but the apparent sample-recurrence-versus-T-cell-expansion contrast is restricted to 11 selected peptides, has ambiguous sample identifiers for some rows, lacks donor identifiers, and is not shown to be a novel biological increment. The Science PDO/TCR contrast is also directly relevant but already published. Do not fit a model or begin lab work from these tables.
