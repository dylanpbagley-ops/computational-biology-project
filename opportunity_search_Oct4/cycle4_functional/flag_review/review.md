## Decision

**Advance MED16 only to one narrowly prespecified validation gate; do not describe the current result as a substantial extension or as a modality effect.** The signal is the strongest of five because the same exact-symbol perturbation is sign-stable sensitizing in three Lawson models at both earlier and later CTL sampling, while the Ramos selected panel reports resistance-direction enrichment at both in-vitro E:T ratios. The effect directions and source screens are already published inputs, however, and the current comparison joins one mouse tumor-screen collection to one separate B-ALL CAR-T study. Tumor lineage/background, antigen, protocol, assay timing and effector modality are not independently varied. The result is a descriptive cross-study contrast, not evidence that changing only CTL to CAR-T reverses a gene effect.

The one useful mechanism lead is also already partly occupied by prior art. Kiritsy et al. found MED16 to be a specific regulator of IFNγ-induced CIITA/MHC-II in murine macrophages and showed that its loss impairs CD4 T-cell activation. Their CRISPR screen ranked Med16 sixth; all four guides were enriched in the MHC-II-low fraction, and two-guide knockout follow-up confirmed the IFNγ/Ciita/MHC-II effect. The retrieved excerpt's targeted comparisons showed this requirement was distinct from the other Mediator subunits tested. That makes “MED16 is a new IFNγ or antigen-presentation regulator” an untenable novelty claim. It does **not** test tumor-cell MHC-I/CD8 recognition, CD19 abundance, or CAR-T killing. Thus the paper supplies a plausible hypothesis boundary, not an explanation for the observed cross-study reversal.

At most one next gate is warranted, and only if an independent tumor-side perturbation dataset can test MED16's functional effect with actual measured target-antigen expression and baseline-fitness controls; a matched CTL/CAR-T system would best separate effector recognition from study differences. The macrophage study does not show that MED16 alters tumor-cell MHC-I/CD8 presentation or CD19, so those remain untested possibilities, not mechanisms inferred here. Mere replication of opposite signs across existing papers remains a modest reanalysis. The inspected flag table, count matrix and Ramos gene-level workbook do not contain these required functional antigen/fitness readouts. Whether an independent computationally accessible dataset contains them was not checked, so availability is **unverified**. The current evidence alone does not justify launching the extension; pursue only the single gate if the needed independent data can be established in a later scoped audit; otherwise stop.

## Gene-by-gene boundary

- **MED16:** Lawson's in-vivo EMT6 sensitizer result is explicitly reported in the source Results, and the pilot reconstructs a broader three-model earlier/later CTL sensitization pattern from deposited counts. The Ramos workbook already publishes its two positive LFC/FDR CAR-T outcomes. No exact Lawson-to-Ramos comparison surfaced in this bounded targeted search. MED16's macrophage IFNγ–CIITA–MHC-II role is known; any tumor-cell MHC-I/CD8 or CAR-T mechanism is untested and cannot be inferred from it. Strongest candidate, with the one-gate condition above.
- **NPRL3:** The source effects are present in the Lawson count resource and Ramos selected-panel workbook, but the pilot's earlier/later CTL direction is stable in only two contexts. No gene-specific CAR-T or cytotoxic-T primary result appeared in the ten titles screened for the alternative genes. A specific immune mechanism is not established by these data or this search. Do not advance independently of the MED16 gate.
- **CBFA2T3:** The source effects are likewise already in the two published data resources, but its earlier/later CTL sensitization is stable in zero contexts, despite the Ramos workbook flag. Primary leukemia literature establishes a role in hematopoietic progenitors and PRDM14-driven T-ALL; another primary study links MTG16/CBFA2T3 loss to epithelial injury and Th1 inflammation. These are important lineage/fitness and immune-context facts, not evidence for CAR-T transport. Do not advance.
- **ETV5:** The source outcomes are already published and CTL sensitization is earlier/later stable in two contexts. A 2025 HCC study reports tumor ETV5 promoting PD-L1/S100A9 and PMN-MDSC immunosuppression, but not this CTL-versus-CD19-CAR-T perturbation contrast. That known suppressive biology makes immune-context effects plausible; it does not explain the selected-panel direction. Do not advance over MED16 on this evidence.
- **IRF1:** The source data contain the opposing pathway behavior already considered in the frozen prior-art review; it is a known IFN-response node with line-dependent CTL effects. The Ramos workbook's outcome is published. Generic IFN/context reversal is established prior art, so this is not a distinct candidate absent a precise, nonduplicative contrast. No such contrast was established here.
