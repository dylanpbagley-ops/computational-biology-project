# Cycle 1 opportunity map

## Recommendation

No question is ready to become a project. **C02 (early killing versus regrowth) and C05 (natural peptide presentation versus patient-matched T-cell recognition) are the two biologically stronger questions for a bounded feasibility test.** Both currently fail a key data/prior-art gate, so this does not select either as a project. C01 is the only lead with preview-row/schema evidence and is useful as an assay-feasibility benchmark, but its biological contribution is modest. C08 has a clinically interpretable endpoint but unresolved case-level access and small cohorts. The ranked question cards in `candidates.tsv` distinguish biological importance from readiness.

## What the literature closes and leaves uncertain

**PDAC functional testing.** HOPE directly reports limited feasibility: 12 of 76 participants achieved testing, with a median 96-day turnaround (35–265 days). Its official 20.91-kB Figshare workbook contains specimen, biopsy, growth-status and timing fields; the native preview exposed the first 49 biopsy records and stated that the workbook has more than 50 rows. The full workbook row count and any response-linked join were not verified. Growth predictors are already reported, so C01’s only plausible increment is usable result returned within a treatment decision window, including failures and timing. Practical usefulness is clear; biological novelty is modest.

The 2025 paper *Multi-drug pharmacotyping improves therapy prediction in pancreatic cancer organoids* (DOI 10.1186/s12935-025-03969-7) is a direct competitor to combination-score ideas. It tested 43 patients, established PDOs in 25, matched first-line responses in 13 cases, and reported 11/13 matches for multi-drug AUC clustering versus 10/13 for a summation/PK score. The assay used 3-day CellTiter-Glo; replicate wells are technical, not patient units. This closes generic “improve AUC/combination scoring” claims. It does not by itself settle whether assay/clinical concordance differs between metastatic RECIST response and post-resection relapse, which motivates C08. Its data statement points to article and supplement; no case-level outcome file was found.

**AML apoptosis.** The 2025 dynamic-BH3 study is a bioRxiv preprint, not peer reviewed (DOI 10.1101/2025.10.14.682414). Its abstract reports a prospective fixed-panel assay in 92 patients, within-patient CD123-subpopulation priming differences, and remission prediction. That directly overlaps a broad “subpopulation priming predicts response” proposal (C03); a different claim needs independent data and a mechanism not already tested.

**Therapy-driven cell states.** The peer-reviewed 2026 scCT-DB paper (DOI 10.1093/nar/gkaf1065) reports 266 paired datasets, 6.19 million cells from 1,142 samples, 27 cancers and direct response-versus-nonresponse plus longitudinal comparison modules. It closes generic cross-cancer pre/post state-signature proposals (C07). Expression shifts are not lineage tracing. The database portal timed out here, so the scale is paper-reported and no file schema was inspected.

**T-cell recognition and CAR-T relapse.** TESLA reports measured patient-matched T-cell recognition, while later NeoScore and Neo-intline work use overlapping benchmarks. Its “presented” label is a model filter (predicted HLA binding, tumor expression and predicted stability), not direct mass-spectrometry evidence of natural tumor peptide presentation. BigMHC (2023; DOI 10.1038/s42256-023-00694-6) is relevant prior work because it trains on mass-spectrometry presentation and transfers to immunogenicity; whether linked natural-presentation and T-cell assays support C05 remains unresolved. For CAR-T, a single-patient B-ALL study reports pre-existing CD19-negative subclones, a mouse study demonstrates lineage switch under CAR pressure, an infusion-product paper studies CD19-positive relapse, and Project EVOLVE is a 2025 international lineage-switch analysis. Broad antigen-escape or product-state claims are therefore already occupied; no public paired case-level resource was inspected for a narrower increment (C04/C06).

## Candidate cards

- **C01 — PDAC assay feasibility (data-access benchmark).** Does biopsy quality and modality predict a patient-level usable result before treatment selection? HOPE provides real specimen/timing fields, but only 12/76 tested and no verified outcome join; a clinical decision question may remain if full records and a second cohort exist. Its contribution is mainly practical feasibility, so it is not the biological lead.
- **C02 — early killing versus regrowth (biological-priority lead; data gate fails).** Does cell-resolved early death commitment relate to later same-sample regrowth after drug washout across medically relevant patient-derived cancer models? No matched processed dataset was found. Bulk ATP and growth-rate-normalized GR are useful comparators only where actually measured; without them, no incremental-superiority claim is supported. Before novelty can be judged, compare against published growth-rate inhibition (Hafner et al., 2016), fractional-killing/death metrics, and delayed-response/washout studies; exact primary citations and evidence were not verified in this pass. This could be a biological study without a clinical cohort, but data or experimental access must be established first.
- **C03 — AML dynamic-BH3 heterogeneity.** Directly overlapped by the 2025 unreviewed preprint on 92 patients, CD123 subpopulations and remission prediction; do not advance in its broad form.
- **C04 — pre-existing clone versus lineage conversion.** Important mechanism, with patient, mouse and 2025 international studies in the field. The exact ancestry increment remains unresolved because Project EVOLVE was not close-read and no paired case-level data were inspected; require a precise subgroup and genetic lineage evidence.
- **C05 — natural neoantigen presentation versus TCR recognition.** Potentially important mechanistic distinction; TESLA's presentation label is model-derived, not direct mass spectrometry. Actual patient-grouped rows, a linked natural-presentation/T-cell dataset and later-model overlap remain unresolved. Reserve for an access/prior-art check only.
- **C06 — CAR-T product state conditional on tumor antigen.** The closest infusion-product study directly addresses CD19-positive relapse; no independent paired product/tumor cohort was identified. Reject as stated.
- **C07 — cross-cancer post-treatment state signatures.** scCT-DB directly includes this comparison class; reject the generic version. A named mechanism with genetic/perturbation evidence would be a new question.
- **C08 — PDAC outcome-context discordance (provisional lead).** Does concordance differ between metastatic RECIST response and resected-disease relapse, with testing delay as a modifier? Clinically interpretable and narrower than score tuning, but row access, cohort sizes and independent replication are unknown.

## Feasibility-review priorities and decisive checks

**Biological priority — C02:** check for processed cancer-model data with matched early cell death and post-washout regrowth, allowing organoid or other patient-derived systems. Bulk ATP/GR should be compared only if measured; its absence limits an assay-superiority claim, not the biological question about regrowth. If no usable data exist, the parent can separately decide whether experimental access is realistic. Before calling it new, verify growth-rate inhibition (Hafner et al., 2016), fractional-killing/death metrics and delayed-response/washout/rechallenge work; those comparator references were not fully verified in this pass.

**Biological priority — C05:** inspect the smallest TESLA/Synapse file for patient keys and separate direct natural-presentation and T-cell recognition layers, then compare the exact training/evaluation overlap in later predictors. Do not infer natural presentation from binding affinity or infer an open gap from paper-level 608-tested/37-positive counts.

**Data-access benchmark — C01:** verify the entire HOPE workbook row count, subject-to-biopsy map, time-to-test distribution, decision/treatment dates and failure status; compare with an independent PDO cohort. If no treatment-window outcome is present, keep it descriptive or stop.

**Lower-priority clinical check — C08:** verify case-level clinical labels and PDO results in the 2025 supplement, then establish the exact identity, endpoint and sample grouping of the 2024 advanced-PDAC study and later PMC11876464 lead. Require enough patients per clinical setting and a separate cohort before analysis.

## Negative findings and access limits

The search used the plan’s 24 broad queries and 6 targeted gap-fill/citation checks; this is not exhaustive. Six reviews were mapped, but only two (ex vivo response and apoptosis) had substantive full-text sections read; four were restricted to indexed review excerpts/abstract-level material. Twenty-one primary/preprint/resource records were screened; one (P20) received a full main-text read, while the rest were targeted, abstract, metadata or repository checks and are marked in `papers.tsv`. This falls short of the intended 8–12 close reads and is reported rather than padded.

Ten data cards record official resources/leads. Only the HOPE workbook yielded actual preview rows and schema; preview truncation means no full row count. TESLA, AML, Beat AML and GEO sources are metadata-only; scCT-DB and Pan-PreClinical file access was blocked or unavailable. The PROSPERO primary paper (2023; PMID 37171617) already compares acute markers with later viability, but its small public supplements were identified by the parent and not inspected here; it does not by itself resolve post-washout reproductive regrowth. No files were downloaded. Pan-PreClinical is a 2026-02-17 bioRxiv preprint (DOI 10.64898/2026.02.14.705918), not a peer-reviewed resource; its counts remain claims from the paper until public files are verified. No clinical predictions, independent validation, biological experiment or pilot were performed.
