# Original cycle3 closeout (pre-correction; retained for history)

## Verdict

**stop_current_question** as a pilot candidate from this bounded cycle. The state-conditioned peptide-presentation question is biologically meaningful, but the one directly paired nonmalignant resource identified here does not provide public peptide rows for the required sequence-level join and independent evaluation. The eight focused searches did not identify an alternate paired normal-cell perturbation resource. This is a bounded access/evaluability stop, not a claim that no such public data exist anywhere, not evidence that inflamed nonmalignant cells lack those peptides, and not a novelty refutation.

## Closest prior work and remaining distinction

Clarkson et al. (2026; PMID 42304380; DOI 10.1186/s12974-026-03915-y) directly measured HLA-I ligandomes from human iPSC neural aggregates and matched fibroblasts from four donors after vehicle or IFNγ (100 ng/mL, 72 h). The paper reports thousands of IFNγ-associated peptides and distinct neural, fibroblast, and vehicle-condition repertoires. It separately reports antigen-dependent injury using a forced neuron-specific viral polyepitope; that experiment does not show that a naturally observed candidate tumor peptide caused injury. Its four-page public supplement contains figures and donor HLA types, not peptide-level rows.

The publisher's data-availability statement says the study datasets are not yet publicly available and can be requested from the corresponding author while deposition is being prepared. No accession appears in the article. One targeted exact-DOI search in each of the official PRIDE and MassIVE domains returned no indexed repository record. This is not proof that no deposit exists elsewhere; no author contact or access request was made.

Ely et al. (Science 2025; PMID 40339010; DOI 10.1126/science.adk3487) measured immunopeptidomes from IFNγ-treated PDAC organoids and filtered candidate cryptic antigens against healthy-tissue immunopeptidomic, thymus, and Ribo-seq references. Those references are not paired inflammatory nonmalignant-cell controls. The authors explicitly note that unprofiled normal cell types or states could translate, process, and present the candidates. This motivates the question but does not demonstrate that a different targeting rule is novel or evaluable. Healthy-reference state history is not assumed to be quiescent unless source methods establish it.

Bourne et al. (Blood Advances 2022; PMID 35561310; DOI 10.1182/bloodadvances.2021006069) measured treatment-conditioned HLA ligands in malignant DLBCL lines and selected source proteins using overlap across tumor lines and absence from a published healthy-tissue source list. This is prior art for tumor-state remodeling and healthy-reference filtering, but it did not perturb nonmalignant cells and measure their paired peptide ligandomes.

## Data and evaluation gate

P01 describes vehicle and IFNγ contrasts and four donor-level immunopeptidomic units. However, the public supplement has no ligand table, the publisher identifies no accession, and the bounded official-repository lookups found no indexed entry. The large single-nucleus RNA-seq cell count is not a substitute for measured peptide presentation. Without the actual sequences and sample keys, the JCI cycle2 peptide table cannot be joined to the neural data, and no held-out detection result—positive or negative—can be calculated. Science's processed target files remain inaccessible as already recorded in cycle2; they were not retried.

Therefore the data and evaluation gates fail for the identified direct resource and the bounded alternatives reviewed here. A mass-spectrometry nondetection is not treated as a true negative or a safety result. No one data-backed revision survives, and no pilot or model fitting is recommended. Revisit only if peptide-level rows and condition/donor keys become publicly inspectable, or a separately reviewed plan identifies another accessible paired resource and fixed contrast.
