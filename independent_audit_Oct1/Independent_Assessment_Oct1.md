# Independent project assessment — October 1, 2026

This is advisory assessment material, not student-authored submission text. Original labels, scoring rules, predictions, and project results were not edited. Calculations were specified in `audit_precommitment.md` and hashed before running. Numerical outputs are in `audit_results.json` and `fragility_recheck.json`.

**Overall judgment.** The project has a worthwhile question, a strong retrospective ranking signal, substantial computational checking, and an achievable path to a good science-fair submission. It is not yet a demonstrated general clinical safety predictor. Several current headline interpretations exceed the evidence. The immediate priority is scientific correction and student understanding, rather than another model or a wider project.

**The contribution in plain language.** A targeted cancer treatment may also attack healthy cells carrying its antigen. This project asks whether the pattern of normal expression helps distinguish historically tolerated target-agent examples from examples associated with serious toxicity. It scores each target using the largest cell type's share of expression across 154 HPA cell types, adding one when blood is the largest lineage family. Larger scores mean predicted safer. Its supporting mathematics concerns limits on tumor killing versus weighted normal-cell killing under specified response assumptions. Earlier EV and CAR-T distribution analyses are background to this narrower question.

**The numerical result survives recomputation.** Loading the original HPA archive reproduces all 44 operational genes' stored top shares exactly and reproduces the blood flags without discrepancies. The operational panel contains 19 safe, 11 toxic, 12 efficacy-failure, and two excluded targets. Only the first 30 enter the main safety test. There are 191 concordant and 18 discordant pairs, no ties, out of 209. AUC is 0.9138755981; the exact one-sided Mann–Whitney p-value is 0.0000278615. Confinement alone gives AUC 0.8803827751 and exact p = 0.0001462456. These AUCs describe pairwise ranking, not percentages of drugs classified correctly.

Fresh verification: the documented-number verifier passes 221 checks; the two handoff test files pass all 10 tests; the full tests directory passes 341 tests in 412.44 seconds. The full suite initially could not collect because the audit environment lacked matplotlib; after installing the dependency in the separate audit folder, the complete run passed. Tests ran under bundled Python 3.12 with isolated dependencies, rather than the original Python 3.10 environment. No project source changes were needed. Passing tests establish the checks they implement, not clinical validity or completeness of theorem wording.

With 20,000 stratified bootstrap draws over targets, seed 20261001, the main AUC's 95% interval is 0.789–0.995. Confinement's is 0.727–0.990. The blood flag's paired AUC gain is 0.0335, with interval 0.000–0.1053. The gain is modest and does not establish a large independently validated benefit. These intervals assume the labels and selected panel are correct; they do not include label, selection, or discovery uncertainty.

All published label-flip summary quantities for k = 1 through 6 were reproduced over 768,211 combinations. All 256 weak-label subsets also matched. No three flips remove significance; the fragility index is four. This supports resistance to these particular label changes. It does not validate the clinical endpoint, species, source attribution, or consistency of choosing one agent per gene. Relabeling uncertain examples as the opposite class is also different from finding that an example should never have been eligible.

**The external-validation claim needs correction.** The breast-cancer study supplies independent tumor and normal expression data for the comparator. The confinement predictor still comes from the original HPA data, and its safety outcomes are the original project labels on a subset of targets. It therefore supplies a comparison with a newly calculated predictor, not an independent clinical replication of confinement. The raw autopsy comparator was also traced back to the underlying long-format data in the audit.

On the same nine safe and nine toxic targets, confinement gives AUC 0.7778 and the within-patient tumor-versus-normal comparator 0.5556. Their paired difference is 0.2222, with a bootstrap 95% interval of −0.0864 to +0.5185. The comparison favors confinement numerically but does not establish superiority. A significant association for one predictor and a nonsignificant association for another is not a significant difference between the predictors. This one comparator also does not represent every target-selection practice in the field. The registry's null association with phase-three advancement is a useful control, but does not conclusively exclude target popularity or selection effects.

The genuinely new validation targets provide only four scorable safe and two toxic examples: AUC 0.875, reported exact p = 0.133. The seventh primary target lacked measured expression, so a convention that scores its absence as safety cannot be counted as prespecified validation. The locked list contains 52 predictions, 32 called and 20 no-call. The checked verdicts include zero forward resolutions and five pre-lock resolutions: two hits and three misses. This is inconclusive and descriptively unfavorable; it cannot be counted as successful prospective validation. A precommitted minimum of eight supports withholding a formal conclusion, not treating misses as irrelevant. The handoff reports OSF submission; this audit did not independently verify a public registration URL or timestamp.

**The theorem has a valid core and an important mismatch.** For a shared kill response depending only on antigen, with fixed distributions and normal-compartment weights and positive total weight, combining the weighted normal distributions is valid. Monotone antigen responses are mixtures of antigen thresholds, so their achievable pairs lie in the convex hull of the antigen-threshold ROC. If arbitrary antigen responses are allowed, likelihood-ratio ordering gives the optimum; that response need not increase with antigen.

The weighted verifier enumerates arbitrary subsets of antigen bins. Its 16,400 checks pass again, but they validate the broader arbitrary-response optimum. They do not establish that every point on that frontier is attainable by a therapy required to increase killing with antigen. In the precommitted example pT=(.45,.10,.45), pN=(.10,.80,.10), a response killing low and high bins but sparing the middle kills 90% of tumor and 20% of normal cells. The monotone antigen ROC hull allows only 51.1% tumor killing at that normal-kill level. The broad optimum is a valid upper bound for monotone therapies, but can be loose.

AUC also does not determine the whole frontier. With uniform normal mass on four antigen bins, both a uniform tumor distribution and tumor mass (.45,.05,.05,.45) have antigen-ranking AUC 0.5. At 25% normal killing, their monotone hulls allow 25% versus 45% tumor killing. Thus the current assertion that the trade-off is governed by a single AUC is false. The full ROC shape matters. These are formulation repairs, not reasons to discard the observed clinical-label association.

The breadth lemma gives a useful elementary worst-case bound on the number of cell types above a chosen fraction of peak expression. It does not itself prove clinical safety, identify critical tissues, or supply a tumor-specific threshold. Its practical interpretation depends on reference partitioning, abundance, exposure, and antigen recognition. The general likelihood-ratio principle is classical; novelty should be claimed in the biological application and empirical evaluation, not as invention of that statistical principle.

**Clinical labels are the largest scientific weakness.** The frozen target table calls FAP marrow damage clinical history, but the primary source matching that damage is a mouse experiment. Mirvetuximab's ocular boxed warning establishes serious drug toxicity, but not on-target attribution: a primary mechanistic study found FRα absent from human corneal tissue and observations consistent with off-target corneal effects. CEACAM5's Parkhurst agent is a TCR-T, not the CAR-T recorded in the table. The project's own Round 7 source review flags additional attribution and severity problems, including EGFR, EPCAM, NCAM1, CD47, and format-dependent ERBB2. This audit is a targeted primary-source review, not a completed audit of all 30 labels.

The differences between agents are biologically meaningful. An approved antibody, a payload-carrying ADC, and a CAR-T aimed at the same gene need not share the same toxicity. Dose, route, tumor indication, and supportive care matter. The simple rule's association might include modality and disease-lineage effects. Freezing a table preserves the historical record; it does not make incorrect source statements reliable. Leave-one-out deletion, label permutations, and local hashes also do not supply independent outcome validation.

**Novelty and impact are narrower than the earlier assessment suggests.** Published work already uses single-cell normal-tissue expression for target safety, including a 2021 normal-tissue CAR-target atlas and SCAN-ACT. The latter integrates normal RNA/protein references and explicitly cautions against extrapolating toxicity across modalities. A potentially distinctive contribution here is a simple normal-only ranking evaluated against curated outcomes, with transparent sensitivity analysis and a mathematically stated scope. The search conducted for this audit is not an exhaustive novelty proof. The strongest plausible practical use is a research prioritization aid. Predicting an individual patient's toxicity or replacing clinical evaluation is unsupported.

**Research-history interpretation matters.** Freezing this rule before trying 74 alternatives is useful discipline, but does not make its original retrospective development outcome-blind. The results themselves discuss CEACAM5 as the motivation for the second factor, and the locked-call thresholds were chosen from the observed panel. The primary result should therefore be described as a discovery-panel association unless an independently dated record supports something stronger. The inspected relative-exposure family-wise correction covers its nine-variant family; it is not a global correction over all 74 alternatives. The count should remain disclosed without claiming broader error control than implemented.

**Record inconsistencies found.** The unchanged frozen TSV has 40 targets, while operational `target_labels.py` has 44; AXL, ERBB3, F3, and GPNMB are absent from the TSV. All eight inspected precommit hashes match, including the stale TSV. The current skeleton's 16 misordered pairs should be 18. The handoff reverses the AUC direction: the 0.914 quantity is safe outranking toxic. The skeleton in `Poster, Paper, and Write Up` is still the September 10 version, while `ev_capture_model/docs` has the September 29 version. The current results document and theorem code retain stale assertions despite correction boxes. The numerical verifier partly checks that a formatted value occurs somewhere in the document; passing is not a complete contextual claim audit.

**Prospects for the stated goals.** Completing a defensible project for the January school fair is very achievable if source review, theorem wording, student-authored writing, and paperwork begin promptly. Qualification through AzSEF is a credible ambition, but the current evidence does not justify calling it likely with an 84–85% probability. An ISEF Grand Award would be a realistic stretch after qualification and strong correction, not a 72–74% expectation derived from these files. First Award and major top awards remain long shots. Prize statistics and titles of previous winners cannot calibrate individual probabilities. Publication could be plausible as an exploratory computational study after substantive review; a high-impact clinically validated result requires more than the current record. The fair outcomes also depend on the student's independently demonstrable contribution and understanding, which files alone cannot assess.
